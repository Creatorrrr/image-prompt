A solitary young adult woman stands in a ruined cathedral on the Moon, her figure and clothing powdered with silver-gray lunar dust. Both boots rest on the regolith-covered floor, her arms hang loosely at her sides, and her face turns toward the distant Earth with a quiet, exhausted expression. She wears a tattered white dress with a frayed lace collar, torn outer skirt panels and a long dust-stained train resting behind her heels. Fine moon dust clings to her cheekbones, loose hair strands and the creases of the fabric. Behind her, a weathered stone saint with a fractured halo occupies a pointed Gothic niche; broken rib vaults and slender tracery frame her like a sacred icon. The cathedral opens onto a barren lunar plain, with the blue crescent of Earth hanging above its distant rim and a tiny derelict orbital vessel silhouetted far beyond the ruins. Fierce sunlight entering through a broken arch produces intense optical lens flare: a white-gold starburst, long horizontal streaks and translucent amber ghosts sweep across the surrounding darkness while her face and the shredded dress remain legible. Wide environmental portrait, the woman small beneath the towering architecture, long empty floor receding around her. The full figure and entire trailing hem fit inside the frame, while the broken arch continues beyond its upper edge. Her silhouette sits in the lower-left third; the stone saint rises farther back on the right, separated from her by an empty strip of nave. Pearl white, ash gray and deep space black, punctuated by cold Earth blue and burning gold. Photorealistic cinematic space opera with Gothic devotional imagery, tangible stone and fabric textures, restrained film grain, a sense of sacred grandeur and abandonment.
