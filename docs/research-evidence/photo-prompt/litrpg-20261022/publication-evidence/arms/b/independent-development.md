# Arm B independent direction before candidate access

Random selection: glassblower-appraiser, from five independently written everyday professions. See random-selection.json.

Three coherent realizations of the selected premise were compared without local candidates:
1. Furnace mouth: dramatic hot glass pulling, artisan job progress above the tool. Strong craft but appraisal details, customer response, and safe visible contact compete with the furnace.
2. Cooled appraisal at the counter: the maker tests a lantern vial for a marsh surveyor. A white internal seed that initially seems a defect is tied to a useful mist-sight stat, and the buyer revises the payment. Strongest single-frame causal chain, with cold glass readable at portrait-plus-worktable scale.
3. Hanging market lantern: the surveyor tests the purchased vial against exterior fog. Clear effect but weaker evidence of actual craft and item trading.

Selected baseline direction: option 2. Fictional woman glassblower-appraiser uses supplied image only for face/hair appearance. Her age, job, mood, history and the other customer are independently fictional and are not inferred from the reference. The initial intent is concentrated curiosity and a small social revaluation of an apparently flawed handmade thing. Thin pale linen and soft side light give subtle human appeal at saved sensual=1 if that is the resolver output; all saved controls will be bound before baseline finalization.

Planned scene: cooled blue glass lantern vial on dark stone worktop; maker and surveyor both within a plausible worktable reach; maker left thumb/index steady the foot, surveyor right hand places a short stack of silver coins while watching an item-linked appraisal pane. Work tools/furnace are secondary place evidence. A concise item card states rarity, effect and assessed price; the artisan job progress has a distinct location and owner. The image should first read as a grounded craft transaction, then reward attention with explicit RPG mechanics. Final scene gates will be registered before the sole image call.

Visibility plan: landscape medium two-person worktable view with item and two faces on a usable focal plane. The vial, its leader-line endpoint, the full text card, the coin contact and the artisan job badge must fit together; no offscreen endpoint can count as evidence. The appraisal card needs enough area for a few short labels; avoid a wall of HUD text. Exact numbers chosen by the author may evolve after retrieval and are not requester locks.
