# Source research: outward visual representation of the fictional yandere archetype

Research date: 2026-08-18  
Scope: general visual construction, with a separate photorealistic translation layer. No project prompts, runtime data, tests, or generated images were consulted. No source images are reproduced here.

## Bottom line

The best-supported core is **a target-conditioned relationship, not a fixed appearance**: unusually concentrated affection toward one person, an expectation of exclusivity or possession, abandonment sensitivity, monitoring/constraint, and a marked reaction when that person's attention is withdrawn or shared. A pleasant ordinary face, a tearful face, a manic grin, blood, a weapon, red light, or dark eye shadows can each occur without that relationship and therefore cannot identify the archetype alone.

No source found establishes a universal or necessary yandere face, gaze, costume, color, or posture. The outward cues below are therefore labeled as **relational core**, **documented drawn convention**, **general horror shorthand**, or **photographic inference**. “Documented” does not mean statistically common unless a corpus is explicitly cited.

## 1. Defining layer: target-specific affection and possessiveness

- **Favored target:** affection is directed to one identified person rather than expressed as generic romance or generic mania. The official *Future Diary* page describes Yuno as an admired, high-performing student whose blind love is specifically for Yukiteru; her diary tracks his actions at ten-minute intervals. This supports target fixation plus monitoring, while also showing that ordinary attractiveness and social competence can be the baseline exterior.
- **Exclusivity / abandonment sensitivity:** Hiyama, Egusa, and Akagi's exploratory “yandere tendency” model retained emotional support, desire to possess, fear of abandonment, and self-sacrifice. Its concrete items include checking a partner's schedule and associates, reserving one's own time for the partner, and service behaviors. These are useful relational dimensions, not validated visual markers.
- **Target-contingent change:** Yanagi and Yonezawa's virtual-agent proposal makes the favored user's identity, gaze, and distance inputs to the agent's affect. It shows pleasure only toward that user, seeks mutual gaze, reacts when the user looks away, and ignores or dislikes non-target users. This conditional contrast is more diagnostic than any one face.
- **Violence is escalation, not the semantic root:** the official *Happy Sugar Life* introduction couples a specific beloved and shared domestic life with blackmail, confinement, and murder when that life is threatened. This is strong evidence for one work's affection-to-harm escalation, but not evidence that every yandere depiction needs gore or a weapon.

## 2. Outward face, gaze, and posture conventions

These are usable conventions when tied to the relational layer above; none is sufficient alone.

| Channel | Observable construction | What it can communicate | Evidence status and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target-locked gaze | Eyes, head, and attention repeatedly converge on the same target; little visual engagement with bystanders | Selective attention / fixation | Directly designed into the HAI agent. Real-person studies connect gaze to liking, but do not establish possessiveness. |
| Mutual-gaze seeking | Character leans or reorients to recover eye contact when the target looks away | Need for reciprocation; target-conditioned arousal | HAI prototype behavior. Kellerman et al. found that sustained mutual gaze could increase reported liking/love in laboratory dyads; this supports affection legibility only. |
| Relational immediacy | Smaller distance, forward lean, head/body orientation toward the target, smile | Affiliation and approach | Wada found liking effects across lean, smile, head orientation, eye contact, and looking, but in only 24 male undergraduates. These are generic liking cues, not yandere cues. |
| Favored-target pleasure (drawn) | Heart-shaped pupils, raised mouth corners, cheek blush | Intensified “dere” toward the target | Explicit choices in the HAI paper, not a reception-validated standard. Literal heart pupils are a graphic symbol, not human anatomy. |
| Withdrawal/rival distress (drawn) | Teary eyes, lowered mouth corners, face shadow; abrupt loss of the prior pleasant expression | Abandonment fear or displeasure when exclusivity is threatened | Explicit HAI mapping. The authors state that the mapping still requires objective validation and that appearance confounded their design. |
| Manic-euphoric face (drawn) | Spiral irises, asymmetric grin, exaggerated tear lines | High-arousal unsettling affect | Parrillo et al. assigned these cues to a “Manic–Euphoric” class and equated that class with yandere imagery. This records one annotation practice, not an archetype definition: one annotator labeled a Pinterest-containing dataset, with no inter-rater agreement or artist-disjoint split. |
| Ordinary public baseline | Polished, attractive, shy, friendly, or neutral presentation before a target-triggered contrast | Concealment and contrast | The official *Future Diary* profile supplies one clear case. It establishes possibility, not prevalence. |

Two negative findings matter:

1. Hart's review notes that researchers do not know a facial expression unique to jealousy; context is needed to distinguish it from anger, sadness, fear, or mixed affect.
2. Direct gaze plus anger makes a face look more self-directed and angry in Ewbank et al., but that communicates **threat**, not affection or possessiveness. Pairing it with a visible favored target or exclusivity trigger is essential.

## 3. Horror shorthand: useful mood, non-defining semantics

- **Face shadow, black background, vertical gloom lines, and zoom/impact lines** belong to a wider manga visual vocabulary. Cohn and Ehly coded 73 graphic schemas across 20 manga volumes and found genre-dependent proportions; their analysis does not make any of these yandere-specific.
- **Spiral eyes, extreme grin, tear streaks, blood spatter, weapons, Dutch tilt, and red/black grading** can quickly signal mania, violence, or horror. Of these, the cited yandere-labeled image study directly documents only spirals, asymmetric grins, and exaggerated tear lines. Blood, weapons, and tilted framing were not frequency-tested here and should be treated as production shorthand rather than a sourced yandere norm.
- **Red is at most an amplifier.** Controlled work by Peromaa and Olkkonen found that facial or surrounding red shifted anger perception only slightly (about 3–4 morph percentage points), with a robust effect in only a minority of participants. A red wash cannot carry the archetype.
- **Low-key lighting is mood rather than identity.** Poland's experiment found more mystery, suspense, malice, and unease under low-key lighting, but also some positive responses; it was a master's thesis using a limited stimulus design. Use low-key contrast to set genre, not to substitute for target-specific behavior.

Practical rule: add horror codes only **after** the image already shows who is loved, what exclusivity is wanted, or what interruption is being resisted. Otherwise the result is merely “attractive horror character,” “stalker,” “killer,” or “manic face.”

## 4. Photorealistic translation limits

1. **Translate graphic morphemes, do not literalize them.** Cohn and Ehly show that hearts, face shadows, background effects, and other manga signs are conventional form–meaning units whose use varies by visual-language “dialect.” In a photograph, heart pupils or spiral irises read as contacts/VFX; vertical gloom lines read as graphic overlay. Substitute motivated human and cinematic cues.
2. **A single isolated face loses the defining conditionality.** The HAI proposal represents the archetype through changes caused by target identity, gaze, and distance. A still portrait cannot show a state transition unless it includes the target, a rival/withdrawal event, or an unmistakable target-linked artifact.
3. **Real facial affect remains ambiguous.** Moist lower eyelids can read as sadness, direct gaze as intimacy or threat, and a smile as friendliness. There is no known jealousy-specific face. Require at least two independent semantic channels.
4. **Do not turn a fictional trope into a diagnosis.** None of the cited work establishes a clinical “look,” and the exploratory yandere scale is not a diagnostic instrument. Avoid claims that eye shape, under-eye darkness, grooming, gender, or attractiveness reveals mental illness.
5. **Camera-facing direct gaze changes the target.** If the viewer is meant to be the beloved, first-person framing can work, but a target-linked object or interaction should confirm it. If a separate beloved is in frame, orient eyes, head, torso, and proximity consistently toward that person rather than toward the camera.

### Drawn-to-photo mapping

| Drawn cue | Photographic substitute | Residual ambiguity |
|---|---|---|
| Heart pupils + blush + smile | Natural catchlights, sustained focus on the target, small genuine-looking smile, mild cheek warmth, forward-oriented posture | Reads as ordinary attraction unless exclusivity is also visible. |
| Spiral iris / “crazy eyes” | Keep normal anatomy; use unusually sustained gaze, low blink implication, and a slight mismatch between warm mouth and tense eyes | Still reads as intensity or threat without target context. “Low blink” is a directing inference, not a yandere-specific research result. |
| Face shadow / black background | Motivated low-key or side light while retaining readable eyes; isolate subject/target with depth of field or negative space | Conveys suspense broadly. |
| Tears + downturned mouth after target withdrawal | Moist lower lids, compressed or falling mouth corners, forward lean toward a target who is turning away | Communicates distress; the visible withdrawal makes abandonment sensitivity legible. |
| Rapid cute-to-hostile switch | Use a diptych/sequence; in one frame, show the exact interruption and a transitional expression rather than two incompatible full expressions | A single frame can only imply, not demonstrate, change over time. |
| Graphic hearts surrounding the pair | Concrete dyadic staging: selective proximity, aligned body orientation, personal gift/photo/phone trace tied to one person | Props can become clutter or generic romance unless monitoring/exclusivity is clear. |

## Reusable cue taxonomy and prompt bundles

### Required semantic channels

Use at least **A + B**. C and D are optional.

- **A — Target designation (required):** identify the beloved in-frame or by a concrete target-linked artifact; converge gaze, head, torso, and spatial attention on that target.
- **B — Exclusivity / loss condition (required):** show monitoring, guarded access, a rival receiving attention, the target turning away, or a self-sacrificial service act. This channel answers “why is this more than attraction?”
- **C — Affect display (optional):** restrained warmth toward the target; strained smile, tears, or anger when attention is lost. The affect must be caused by B.
- **D — Genre overlay (optional):** low-key lighting, face shadow, red accent, ominous negative space, blood, or weapon. D changes tone but does not define the archetype.

### Bundle 1: non-horror possessive devotion

`A`: favored target visibly present; the character's eyes, head, torso, and slight forward lean all track that one person.  
`B`: the character has positioned herself/himself closer than anyone else and holds a target-specific gift, schedule, or message thread; another person's attempt to interrupt is visible but receives no attention.  
`C`: warm, controlled smile and mild cheek warmth only toward the target.  
Result: devotion plus exclusivity, without requiring violence.

### Bundle 2: attention-withdrawal transition

`A`: target turns toward a third person while the character remains fully oriented to the target.  
`B`: closing distance or reaching to recover attention; a phone notification, conversation partner, or departing gesture makes the loss cue explicit.  
`C`: smile beginning to tighten, moist lower lids or lowered mouth corners, direct target-locked gaze.  
`D` optional: restrained side shadow or a small red accent.  
Result: abandonment sensitivity and possessiveness are visible in one frame; the facial expression is contextual rather than diagnostic.

### Bundle 3: ordinary mask / private fixation

`A`: ordinary polished public presentation and an identifiable beloved.  
`B`: a private, target-specific monitoring trace in the same scene (repeated timestamped messages, meticulously logged schedule, duplicated photos, gifts prepared only for that person).  
`C`: pleasant public smile while attention remains disproportionately fixed on the beloved.  
Result: normal exterior contrasted with relational excess. Avoid illegible on-screen text; the object pattern should remain understandable without reading small type.

### Bundle 4: horror escalation

Start with Bundle 1 or 2, then add only one or two D cues: motivated low-key light, face shadow, a limited red accent, an aftermath trace, or a weapon held secondary to the target relationship. Keep the target/exclusivity event more compositionally informative than the horror prop.

## Source ledger

1. **Hiyama, Egusa & Akagi (2024), “Relationship between yandere thinking tendency and University Life Satisfaction.”** J-STAGE / Japanese Society for Artificial Intelligence SIG technical report; cross-sectional questionnaire and SEM.  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.11517/jsaisigtwo.2024.BI-024_23  
   Supports: exploratory dimensions of emotional support, possessiveness, abandonment fear, and self-sacrifice; monitoring and schedule-checking items.  
   Limits: 60 respondents with relationship experience from one private university; authors constructed the yandere scale for this study; several proposed factors were dropped for poor measurement; cross-sectional SEM does not establish causality; no visual test.

2. **Yanagi & Yonezawa (2023), “Implementation of Yandere Characteristics for Virtual Agent.”** HAI Symposium short paper / proposed 2D agent implementation.  
   URL: https://hai-conference.net/proceedings/HAI2023/pdf/P-60.pdf  
   Supports: explicit target-identity, gaze, and distance conditioning; mutual-gaze seeking; chosen heart-pupil/blush/smile and tear/downturned-mouth/shadow mappings.  
   Limits: not a validated cue-frequency study; no demonstrated reception accuracy; the authors explicitly call for an objective definition and validation and acknowledge appearance confounding.

3. **TV anime *Future Diary*, official Yuno Gasai character page.** Primary franchise character/profile page.  
   URL: https://future-diary.tv/chara/2nd.html  
   Supports: ordinary admired exterior, blind affection toward one named target, norm-breaking behavior, and ten-minute target monitoring.  
   Limits: one authored character; page does not claim to define the yandere class or estimate prevalence.

4. ***Happy Sugar Life*, VAP official anime page.** Primary distributor/publisher synopsis.  
   URL: https://www.vap.co.jp/happysugarlife/  
   Supports: a named beloved and sweet shared life as motive; threat to that dyad linked to blackmail, confinement, and murder.  
   Limits: promotional synopsis for a self-described psychological-horror work; violence and domestic sweetness may be title-specific.

5. **Cohn & Ehly (2016), “The vocabulary of manga: Visual morphology in dialects of Japanese Visual Language.”** Peer-reviewed corpus study, *Journal of Pragmatics* 92, 17–29.  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2015.11.008  
   Author PDF: https://www.visuallanguagelab.com/P/NCSE_mangamorphology.pdf  
   Supports: conventional graphic schemas as visual morphemes; corpus evidence from 20 volumes for shared and genre-varying morphology, including face shadows, black backgrounds, gloom lines, blush, and other affective devices.  
   Limits: shōnen/shōjo sample only, 1997–2012 works selected via a popularity site; not yandere-specific and explicitly preliminary.

6. **Parrillo, Santoro, Laura & Rughetti (2026), “Facial Expression Recognition in Anime and Manga Characters.”** Peer-reviewed computer-vision benchmark, *Information* 17(5), 484.  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/info17050484  
   Supports: documentation that one dataset construction practice grouped spiral irises, asymmetric grins, and exaggerated tear lines as a visually learnable “Manic–Euphoric” class associated by the authors with yandere imagery.  
   Limits: the class collapses a relational archetype into facial affect; 3,000-image dataset includes Pinterest; a single annotator used internal guidelines; no inter-rater agreement or artist/studio-disjoint split. It cannot support prevalence or necessity claims.

7. **Wada (1988), “Effects of liking, interpersonal distance, and topics on nonverbal behaviors.”** Peer-reviewed experiment, *The Japanese Journal of Psychology* 59(1), 45–52.  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.4992/jjpsy.59.45  
   Supports: liking-related variation in forward lean, smile, head orientation, eye contact, and looking; distance also affected lean, eye contact, and body orientation.  
   Limits: 24 male undergraduates interacting in pairs; generic liking/immediacy, not obsession or yandere recognition.

8. **Kellerman, Lewis & Laird (1989), “Looking and loving: The effects of mutual gaze on feelings of romantic love.”** Peer-reviewed laboratory experiments, *Journal of Research in Personality* 23(2), 145–161.  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-6566(89)90020-2  
   Supports: mutual unbroken gaze can increase reported liking and love under specific laboratory conditions.  
   Limits: strangers in constrained opposite-sex dyads; effects varied by participants' self-perception tendency; says nothing about possessiveness, threat, or still-image recognition.

9. **Ewbank, Jennings & Calder (2009), “Why are you angry with me? Facial expressions of threat influence perception of gaze direction.”** Peer-reviewed primary experiment, *Journal of Vision* 9(12).  
   URL: https://doi.org/10.1167/9.12.16  
   Supports: angry faces with direct gaze are perceived as more directly angry/self-relevant than averted-gaze anger.  
   Limits: supports a threat-reading interaction, not a yandere or affection reading.

10. **Hart (2016), “Proximal Foundations of Jealousy: Expectations of Exclusivity in the Infant's First Year of Life.”** Scholarly review, *Emotion Review* 8(4), 358–366.  
    URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915615431  
    Supports: jealousy is inferred from exclusivity-threatening context and mixed affect/behavior; no unique jealousy facial expression is known.  
    Limits: review focus is infant-caregiver jealousy, not adult romance, fiction, or yandere design; use only for the facial-ambiguity/context principle.

11. **Peromaa & Olkkonen (2019), “Red color facilitates the detection of facial anger — But how much?”** Peer-reviewed psychophysics study, *PLOS ONE* 14(4), e0215610.  
    URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215610  
    Supports: red can slightly bias perceived anger under controlled conditions.  
    Limits: effect was small and robust only for a minority; anger is not yandere; no support for red as a defining palette.

12. **Poland (2015), “Lights, Camera, Emotion!: an Examination on Film Lighting and Its Impact on Audiences' Emotional Response.”** Cleveland State University master's thesis; lighting experiment with 162 participants.  
    URL: https://engagedscholarship.csuohio.edu/etdarchive/379/  
    Supports: low-key lighting can increase suspense, mystery, malice, intrigue, and unease responses.  
    Limits: thesis rather than peer-reviewed journal article; limited film stimuli; also elicited some positive emotion; not yandere-specific.

## Evidence-safe conclusion

For a photo prompt, the most defensible visual formula is:

> **identified beloved + convergent gaze/head/body/proximity + visible exclusivity or withdrawal trigger + target-caused affect**, with horror styling added only as an optional overlay.

This formula preserves the relational meaning supported by the strongest sources and avoids confusing the archetype with generic attractiveness, jealousy, stalking, mania, or horror.
