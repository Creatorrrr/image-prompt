L126: ## 3.8 Audio and MIDI
L127: Live deals with two types of signals: audio and MIDI. In the digital world, an audio signal is a series of numbers that approximates a continuous waveform. The signal can originate from various sources, including audio from a microphone, a sound synthesized or sampled through software, or a signal delivered to a loudspeaker.
L128: A MIDI signal is a sequence of commands, such as “now play a C4 at mezzo piano.“ MIDI is a symbolic representation of musical material, one that is closer to a written score than to an audio recording. MIDI signals are generated by hardware input devices such as MIDI or USB keyboards or software devices.
L129: It takes an 【61†instrument】 to convert MIDI signals into audio signals that can actually be heard. Some instruments, such as Live’s 【62†Simpler】, are for chromatic playing of one sound via the keyboard. Other instruments, such as Live’s 【63†Impulse】, have a different percussion sound assigned to each keyboard key.
L130: Audio signals are recorded and played back using audio tracks, and MIDI signals are recorded and played back using MIDI tracks. The two track types have their own corresponding clip types. Audio clips cannot be added to MIDI tracks and vice versa.
L131: 
L132: You can find more information about inserting, reordering, and deleting audio and MIDI tracks in the 【64†Audio and MIDI Tracks】 section of the Mixing chapter.
L133: ## 3.9 Audio Clips and Samples
L134: 
L135: An audio clip contains a reference to a sample (also known as a “sound file“ or “audio file“) or a compressed sample (such as an MP3 file). The clip contains information that instructs Live where on the computer’s drives to find the sample, what part of the sample to play and how to play it.
L136: When a sample is dragged in from Live’s built-in browser, Live automatically creates a clip to play that sample. Prior to dragging in a sample, one can audition or preview it directly in the browser. When the Browser File Preview button with the headphones icon is toggled on, the preview starts automatically once the sample is selected.
L137: 
L138: 【65†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】A Selected Sample with Audio Preview in the Browser.
L139: Live offers many options for playing samples in exciting new ways, allowing you to create an abundance of new sounds without actually changing the original sample — all the changes are computed in real time, while the sample is played. The respective settings can be found in the 【66†Clip View】, which opens when a clip is double-clicked.
L140: 
L141: 【67†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】An Audio Clip’s Properties as Displayed in the Clip View.
L142: Many powerful manipulations arise from Live’s 【68†warping capabilities】. Warping means changing the speed of sample playback independently from the pitch so as to match the song tempo. The tempo can be adjusted on the fly in the Control Bar’s Tempo field.
L143: 
L144: 【69†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】The Control Bar’s Tempo Field.
L145: The most elementary use of this technique, and one that usually requires no manual setup, is synchronizing sample loops to the chosen tempo. Live’s Auto-Warp algorithm actually makes it easy to line up any sample with the song tempo, such as a recording of a drunken jazz band’s performance. It is also possible to radically change the sonic signature of a sound using extreme warp settings.
L146: ## 3.10 MIDI Clips and MIDI Files
L147: 
L148: A MIDI clip contains musical material in the form of MIDI notes and controller envelopes. When MIDI is imported from a 【70†MIDI file】, the data gets incorporated into the Live Set, and the original file is not referenced thereafter. In Live’s browser, a MIDI file appears with a special icon, and with the .mid file extension.
L149: 
L150: 【71†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】A MIDI File in the Browser.
L151: A MIDI clip’s contents can be 【72†accessed and edited】 via the Clip View, for instance to change a melody or create a drum pattern.
L152: 
L153: 【73†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】A MIDI Clip’s Properties as Displayed in the Clip View.
L154: 
L155: Aside from recording incoming MIDI signals from external devices, Live also allows you to add MIDI notes to clips through 【32†Draw Mode】, 【74†MIDI Tools】 or 【75†audio-to-MIDI converters】.
L156: ## 3.11 Devices
L157: 
L158: A track can contain not only clips but also a 【61†chain of devices】 for processing signals. Double-clicking a track’s title bar brings up the 【66†Device View】, which shows the track’s device chain.
L159: 
L160: 【76†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】The Device View Displaying a MIDI Track’s Device Chain.
L161: Devices that receive and deliver audio signals are called 【77†audio effects】. Audio effects are the only type of device that fits in an audio track or a return track. However, two more types of devices are available for use in MIDI tracks: 【78†MIDI effects】 and 【79†instruments】.
L162: Live’s built-in audio effects, MIDI effects, and instruments are available from the browser. You can add devices to tracks by dragging them from the browser into the Device View, or dragging them onto a Session or Arrangement track. You can also load instruments and effects into a track by selecting them in the browser and pressing `Enter`.
L163: 
L164: 【80†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】Live’s Built-in Devices Are Available from the Browser.
L165: You can also use 【81†plug-in devices】 in Live. VST and Audio Units (macOS only) plug-ins are available from the browser’s Plug-Ins label.
L166: 
L167: 【82†Image†ableton-production.imgix.net】Plug-In Devices Are Available from the Browser’s Plug-Ins Label.
L209: Consider a MIDI track playing a clip. The MIDI signal from the clip is fed into the track’s device chain. There, it is first processed by any number of MIDI effects. A MIDI effect receives and delivers MIDI signals. The last MIDI effect in the chain is followed by an instrument, which receives MIDI and outputs audio. Following the instrument, there can be any number of audio effects — as in an audio track.

L169:

L182: ## 3.13 Scale Awareness
L183: 
L184: Live’s scale options let you set any clip to a scale of your choice, and can also be used to apply scales across Live’s effects and devices. Once you activate Scale Mode for a given clip, effect, or device, it becomes scale aware.
L246: Changing an automated control’s value while not recording is similar to launching a Session clip while the Arrangement is playing: It deactivates the control’s automation (in favor of the new control setting). The control will stop tracking its automation and continue using the new value until the Re-Enable Automation button is pressed or a Session clip that contains automation is launched.
L247: ## 3.19 Clip Envelopes
L248: Envelopes can be found in both tracks and clips. 【125†Clip envelopes】 are used to automate or modulate device and mixer controls. Audio clips have additional clip envelopes to influence the clip’s pitch, volume and more; these can be used to change the melody and rhythm of recorded audio. MIDI clips have extra clip envelopes to represent MIDI controller data.
L249: Clip envelopes can be unlinked from the clip to give them independent loop settings, so that larger movements (like fade-outs) or smaller gestures (like an arpeggio) can be superimposed onto the clip’s material.
