L30: ##### 【5†Career Communities】
L31: # What does a Bandleader do?
L32: A bandleader organizes and leads a performing ensemble. Depending on the group, the bandleader may choose repertoire, create or commission arrangements, hire musicians, run rehearsals, communicate with presenters, manage schedules and budgets, and represent the band onstage and in business discussions. Unlike a music director attached to a larger production or organization, a bandleader is often responsible for both the ensemble’s musical performance and its day-to-day operation.
L33: ### At a Glance
L34: 
L35: Career Path
L36: 
L37: There are no strict educational requirements. Bandleaders typically start as instrumental performers (touring, session, or freelance musicians, orchestra members, etc.), making connections and learning to organize others. Advancement often leads to roles as music directors for bands/orchestras, television bandleaders, or creative directors.
L38: 
L39: Finding Work
L40: Requires proving talent through performance, building a strong reputation for organization and leadership, and using social media, websites, and newsletters for self-promotion. Grants can provide funding for self-managed ensembles.
L41: 
L42: Professional Skills
L43:   * High-level instrumental proficiency
L44:   * Depth of playing
L45:   * Arranging
L46:   * Production
L47:   * Organizing an ensemble: scheduling rehearsals, booking gigs, etc.
L48:   * Music preparation
L49:   * Sound systems/amplification
L50:   * Superior ears for musical detail and nuance
L51:   * Leadership
L52:   * Verbal communication

L31: # What does a Tour Manager do?
L32: 
L33: Tour managers create day sheets that detail each day's schedule and distribute them to the band and crew. If there are media engagements planned—such as a record store appearance, a radio station visit, or an interview—the tour manager is responsible for making sure everyone is where they need to be. Perhaps most importantly, the tour manager manages the tour's finances, keeping a close eye on the money that comes in and out to ensure everything stays within the budget.
L34: ### At a Glance
L35: 
L36: Career Path
L37: 
L38: With experience, connections, and a reputation for good work, tour managers can advance to better-paying jobs with more prominent bands and artists, or join the ranks of a record label or concert promotion company.
L39: 
L40: Finding Work
L41: Tour management is typically freelance work, although the most experienced tour managers may be able to snag in-house positions at record labels. It's vital that aspiring tour managers have proven experience living on the road and managing a creative undertaking.
L42: 
L43: Professional Skills
L44: 
L45:   * Schedule management
L46:   * Personnel management
L47:   * Budgeting
L48:   * Experience touring
L49:   * Proactive communication skills
L50:   * Problem-solving
L51:   * Multitasking and organization
L52:   * Leadership
L53:   * Networking
L54: Interpersonal Skills
L55: Tour managers must be excellent multitaskers with terrific time management and organizational skills. Being proactive and making the most of downtime (e.g., while traveling between tour stops) is extremely important to staying on top of work. The best tour managers are well-prepared for the issues and crises that sometimes arise on tour, and deal with them resourcefully and efficiently. They should also be capable of handling interpersonal conflicts—which occur frequently on tour—with grace.
