One continuous photographic art-performance portrait: an adult art performer conducts a still appearance-and-surface inspection in a glass-roof botanical workroom. She is a fictional 27-year-old woman. Reference guidance comes from the supplied face and hair appearance. Her short dark bob has fine separated bangs, and her calm direct gaze acknowledges the camera. Her head and torso face forward on the same plane; her arms rest loosely beside her body as she stands upright. Each transparent theatrical contact lens bears one tiny opaque crimson heart centered over the visible black pupil region; each surrounding brown iris, sclera and eyelid retains its ordinary eye structure. The artificial lens prints are clearly resolved in both open eyes. A deep-blue abstract pigment marking sits on her own bare lower abdominal skin, its center four centimeters directly below her visible navel, with its entire six-centimeter width above the trouser waistband. The pigment follows the shallow curved skin surface and preserves visible pores and a gentle sideward falloff. This deliberately chosen marking has four disconnected elements: one closed central diamond, two mirrored open C-curves facing the diamond, and one short vertical stem below it. A boxy ivory sleeveless utility top ends above the navel; slate canvas work trousers sit below the marking. The scene records the freshly applied pigment settling. Beside her, a shallow tray on the plant bench holds the matching four cut-paper shapes and a capped blue paint bottle; a small brass plate catches a narrow amber reflection. Diffuse overcast skylight softly separates her from ribbed glass, leaves and worn timber. The crown-to-upper-hips composition gives equal reading space to her face and lower-abdominal marking, with both eyes and skin pigment sharply legible at native resolution. Fine photographic skin texture, neutral color and restrained workshop detail keep this a quiet material experiment.
