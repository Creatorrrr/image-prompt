# Source research: glitch, diegetic reality error, and uncanny mismatch

Research date: 2026-08-22  
Scope: reusable visual-meaning contracts for photographic prompt generation. The runtime registry was not used as evidence, no source artwork is reproduced, and rendered-pixel recognizability remains unverified.

## Bottom line

Three neighboring ideas need separate contracts:

1. **Medium-native glitch:** an image, video, signal, or file process visibly breaks and exposes its own technological structure.
2. **Diegetic reality error:** the recording remains optically clean while the depicted world itself violates exactly one invariant.
3. **Uncanny coherence mismatch:** an almost convincing human or humanlike whole contains one localized realism or response channel that disagrees with the rest.

The shared production principle is **constrained inconsistency**. A successful result needs a coherent baseline, one identifiable failure mechanism, repeated evidence governed by that mechanism, and intact comparison regions. Random artifact accumulation weakens all three concepts.

## 1. Medium-native glitch

### Meaning boundary

Rosa Menkman treats glitch as a rupture in an expected flow and emphasizes that failures reveal a medium's otherwise hidden organization. Her distinction between an operative rupture and a standardized effect also warns that a familiar plug-in look can lose the force of an error. Iman Moradi separately distinguishes a captured or “pure” glitch from intentionally produced glitch-alike imagery and describes recurring formal properties such as fragmentation, linearity, repetition, and complexity.

The reusable visual contract therefore does not prescribe one universal RGB offset. It requires:

- one identified source medium or protocol;
- one artifact family native to a plausible failure in that source;
- a bounded rupture rather than uniform decoration;
- enough content continuity to perceive what expected flow has broken;
- repeated artifact geometry that shares the same technological fingerprint.

Examples include codec macroblocks displaced along motion vectors, CRT horizontal synchronization loss, or scanline timing displacement. These are alternative mechanism families, not ingredients to stack together.

### Contrastive boundary

The following are weak substitutes: uniform chromatic aberration, generic RGB separation, arbitrary scanlines, random static, an unrelated VHS-plus-pixel-plus-portal stack, a software error dialog, or a cleanly photographed world-rule violation.

## 2. Diegetic reality error

### Meaning boundary

This category is an implementation synthesis rather than a single established art-historical label. It combines the medium-visibility boundary above with perception research on shadows, reflections, spatial correspondence, and causal coherence. The camera, file, and display remain credible. The anomaly belongs to the world in front of the camera.

The reusable contract requires:

- a familiar and physically coherent baseline;
- optically clean photographic capture;
- exactly one failed invariant: spatial, temporal, causal, optical, or topological;
- the same measurable consequence across at least three affected surfaces;
- unaffected anatomy, objects, lighting, materials, and perspective that remain coherent;
- an ordinary first read followed by an impossible second read.

Candidate mechanisms remain unordered until composition time:

- **spatial coordinate discontinuity:** every crossed surface shifts by the same offset;
- **temporal phase lag:** several materials show the same delayed state;
- **causal orphan:** a repeated effect remains while its required cause is absent;
- **observer-dependent rendering:** correspondence changes consistently with one viewpoint rule;
- **topological recursion:** one bounded route or surface reconnects according to one impossible topology;
- **semantic binding swap:** one stable visual property is consistently assigned to the wrong object class.

The profile intentionally rejects RGB splitting, codec blocks, scanlines, split-screen seams, portals, magical rifts, and unrelated surreal anomaly stacks as evidence of a diegetic error.

### Generator limitation

Text-to-image systems are known to struggle with exact spatial relations and compositional consistency. The contract therefore uses a render gate rather than assuming that a prompt mentioning “the same offset” proves that pixels actually share that offset. Native-scale inspection is mandatory for cross-surface consistency.

## 3. Uncanny coherence mismatch

### Meaning boundary

Uncanny-valley research supports mismatch-based accounts more strongly than a fixed “creepy face” recipe. Near-human stimuli can become disturbing when appearance, motion, category, affect, or behavioral expectation is inconsistent. The reusable image contract therefore uses a high-coherence baseline plus one localized mismatch and one matched comparison channel.

Usable mismatch families include:

- natural skin and anatomy with eye dynamics at a different realism level;
- a socially plausible smile whose timing contradicts the visible event;
- gaze target and head response that disagree;
- appearance realism and motion implication that disagree;
- a reflection that preserves identity but answers with a fractionally different affect.

Gore, monster anatomy, random facial deformation, horror makeup, or blank eyes alone can create horror, but they do not establish the almost-right comparison that makes this contract inspectable.

## 4. Runtime translation

The registry uses three profile IDs:

| Profile ID | Hard activation | Optional discovery | Required visual proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| `medium_native_glitch` | Narrow labels such as `glitch art`, `signal glitch`, `글리치 아트` | Mechanism descriptions | one source medium, one native failure family, bounded rupture, content continuity |
| `diegetic_reality_invariant_failure` | `reality error`, `reality glitch`, `현실 오류`, `현실 글리치` | world-rule descriptions | clean capture, one invariant, same consequence across surfaces, coherent unaffected regions |
| `uncanny_coherence_mismatch` | `uncanny valley`, `불쾌한 골짜기` | localized mismatch descriptions | near-human baseline, one mismatch channel, matched comparison, coherent remainder |

Broad or ambiguous wording is prevented from silently authoring a hard world mechanism. Exact named requests become hard obligations; component or semantic matches remain opt-in candidate concepts. The `requires_adult_character` field is explicitly false for these general visual concepts, while any separate human-age or safety requirement continues to come from the active request and other contracts.

## 5. Source ledger

1. **Rosa Menkman, _The Glitch Moment(um)_ (Institute of Network Cultures, 2011).**  
   URL: https://networkcultures.org/_uploads/NN%234_RosaMenkman.pdf  
   Supports: rupture of expected flow, medium-specific failure fingerprints, and the distinction between disruptive error and standardized effect.  
   Limits: artistic theory rather than a controlled image-recognition experiment.

2. **Iman Moradi, _Glitch Aesthetics_ (2004).**  
   URL: https://organised.info/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Moradi-Iman-2004-Glitch-Aesthetics.pdf  
   Supports: pure glitch versus glitch-alike and formal properties including fragmentation, linearity, repetition, and complexity.  
   Limits: early thesis taxonomy; not a universal technical classification of every digital failure.

3. **Carleigh Morgan, “Calculated error: glitch art and the digital.”**  
   URL: https://aprja.net/article/view/115426  
   Supports: digital errors remain constrained by hardware and software rather than being visually arbitrary.  
   Limits: critical theory, not a quantitative artifact-frequency study.

4. **Nam June Paik, _Zen for TV_, Smithsonian American Art Museum.**  
   URL: https://americanart.si.edu/artwork/zen-tv-76252  
   Supports: an altered television signal can expose the display system as artistic material.  
   Limits: one artwork and museum interpretation; not a general glitch taxonomy.

5. **Smithsonian American Art Museum, “Nam June Paik and the Wobbulator.”**  
   URL: https://americanart.si.edu/blog/nam-june-paik-wobbulator  
   Supports: signal manipulation tied to television electronics rather than a generic visual overlay.  
   Limits: institutional account of one device and practice.

6. **Electronic Arts Intermix, Takeshi Murata, _Monster Movie_.**  
   URL: https://www.eai.org/titles/monster-movie  
   Supports: compression artifacts and digital processing can become the visible material of moving-image work.  
   Limits: distributor description of one artwork.

7. **JODI, Museum of Modern Art collection-gallery text.**  
   URL: https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Floor2-SectionTexts.pdf  
   Supports: interfaces, code, and apparent system malfunction as medium-reflexive artistic material.  
   Limits: curatorial overview rather than a technical or perceptual experiment.

8. **Justyna Janik, “Glitched Perception: On the Transparency and Visibility of the Video Game Medium” (2016).**  
   URL: https://www.gamephilosophy.org/wp-content/uploads/confmanuscripts/pcg2016/Justyna-Janik-Glitched-Perception-On-the-Transparency-and-Visibility-of-the-Video-Game-Medium.pdf  
   Supports: glitches interrupt transparency and make the medium visible.  
   Limits: game-philosophy analysis; transfer to still photography is an implementation inference.

9. **MacDorman and Chattopadhyay, uncanny-valley appearance and category-mismatch research.**  
   URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00390/pdf  
   Supports: category ambiguity and realism inconsistency as contributors to uncanny responses.  
   Limits: specific stimulus sets and experimental conditions; no universal prompt recipe.

10. **Kätsyri et al., review of uncanny-valley hypotheses.**  
    URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21515639/  
    Supports: evaluation of perceptual mismatch and atypical feature accounts.  
    Limits: review conclusions depend on heterogeneous prior stimuli and methods.

11. **Shadow and reflection perception review.**  
    URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6856028/  
    Supports: shadows and reflections provide correspondence cues that can expose a world-level inconsistency.  
    Limits: does not define “reality error”; its use here is a visual-production inference.

12. **Microsoft Research, benchmark of spatial relationships in text-to-image generation.**  
    URL: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2023/08/Benchmarking_Spatial_Relationships_Text_To_Image_Generation.pdf  
    Supports: spatial-relation fidelity is a known evaluation problem for text-to-image systems.  
    Limits: model and benchmark coverage is time-bound; it does not test this registry's exact prompts.

13. **NeurIPS 2023 spatial-compositional text-to-image benchmark paper.**  
    URL: https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2023/file/f8ad010cdd9143dbb0e9308c093aff24-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf  
    Supports: compositional and spatial correctness require explicit evaluation rather than prompt-only assurance.  
    Limits: benchmark performance cannot stand in for rendered review of a new image.

## 6. Evidence limits

- “Diegetic reality error” is a project-facing operational category assembled from medium theory, perception research, and prompt-engineering constraints; it is not claimed as a canonical scholarly term.
- The mechanism families are candidate choices, not universal definitions or fixed scene recipes.
- Registry validation and candidate-pack routing prove contract plumbing only. They do not prove that an image generator will render the mechanism or that viewers will recognize it.
- Any future claim of visual success requires a saved render, thumbnail and native-scale gates, and separate requesting-user judgment.
