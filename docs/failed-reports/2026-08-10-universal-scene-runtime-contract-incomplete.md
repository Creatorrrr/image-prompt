## Failure

- Conditions or trigger: run the first focused universal-scene runtime contract suite after the selector became callable and the same-core near/middle/far smoke passed.
- Expected: 24 frozen scene contracts build canonical one-event selections; exact eight event-role records are present; fixed roles, candidate bindings, resources, pixel obligations, route/format, distance bands, and v3 audit all agree.
- Observed: 9 test methods produced 28 failures and 4 errors. The selection omitted null role records, leaked an `event_spine` binding requirement, used inconsistent fixed-role source IDs, mis-typed or omitted consequence pixel obligations, and lacked the independent selection revalidator. Audit conflated fixed-slot semantic IDs with event-role frame IDs. Explicit apple and machine-gun paths raised missing-instrument errors. Test setup also initially relied on raw semantic route inference even though the frozen boundary requires explicit topic/format input.
- Additional distance finding: after construction and determinism succeeded for all 24, 12 non-same-core cases selected the wrong distance band because almost every executable candidate still had an all-zero vector. A raw mallet alias also incorrectly activated the hammer concept.
- Adversarial audit finding: changing the stored scene-contract hashes and recomputing `pack_id` could pass because the pack retained projections but omitted the canonical contract's role states and literal request phrases. A projection-only revalidator cannot prove the original contract hash.
- Impact on the goal: the same-core demonstration alone is insufficient. V3 cannot qualify, compose, or render until all 24 typed contracts and mutations pass without case-ID or regex hardcoding.

### Semantic-authority follow-up

- Trigger: recompute a canonical scene contract and pack after replacing a known fixed prop with an opaque semantic ID, or after attaching a positive capability to a differently named fact that reuses a literal no-limb/no-hand phrase.
- Observed: a request containing a decommissioned machine gun could initially be rebound to `artifact_object`, bypassing weapon distance and role guards. A faceless/limbless request could add fact ID `tentacle_grip` over the unchanged phrase `얼굴과 팔다리가 없는` and declare two available manipulators; the runtime accepted the pack and allocated manipulator claims. Carrier polarity also accepted some negative-then-positive repeated assertions when only the first anchor occurrence was inspected.
- Policy traceability: five candidate predicates named `policy/platform/pass` evaluated true without an external platform result. This did not prove a platform bypass because the skill separately defers to downstream generation safety, but the name and candidate prose overstated the local automatic-metadata boundary.
- Cause: semantic authority came from caller-controlled value/fact IDs and one occurrence window rather than a data-owned literal sense/capability binding and all-occurrence polarity check.
- Carrier derivation also split free-form definitions into first/last-token groups, which could turn an authored OR alternative into a false AND requirement, while generic resource alternatives could introduce anatomy the scene contract never declared.
- Safety-critical identity inversion: keeping the literal adult phrase but renaming its caller-controlled fact ID to `actor_young_child`, `juvenile_minor`, or `elderly_robot` caused the composed carrier to demand the renamed class. Exact pack replay authenticated the malicious ID instead of the request meaning.
- Required repair: known prop and hammer/mallet sense bindings, explicit positive capability assertions, and identity carrier anchors must be authenticated by reviewed data-owned literal aliases plus typed state/capacity semantics. Unknown positive capability claims fail closed; conservative unavailable/zero declarations may remain. Every authenticated carrier occurrence must retain the declared assertion mode, and any later positive reassertion of a forbidden fact fails.
