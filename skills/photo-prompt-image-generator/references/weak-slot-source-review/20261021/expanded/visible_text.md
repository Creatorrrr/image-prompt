## Primary research and epistemic limits

- [NPS Preservation Brief 25](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/upload/preservation-brief-25-signs.pdf) describes lettered messages attached to physical supports, painted boards, multi-line signage, glass signs and luminous sign forms. It supports carrier/material distinctions; it does not specify the proposed declaration-preservation contract, any frozen probe word, or a universal typography setting.
- [NPS Harpers Ferry accessibility guidelines](https://www.nps.gov/features/hfc/guidelines/) discusses glyph/word/line spacing and foreground/background contrast for sign readability. We borrow the observable readability concern only, without imposing its institutional typefaces, layout or legal accessibility requirements on photographic scenes.
- [The Met, Loving cup, object 2017.97](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/746095) documents a salt-glazed ceramic cup with an incised date and inscription. It supports the existence of inscription physically incised into ceramic. A modern vessel, arbitrary text, its local position and preservation are authored arrangements; date, dedication and historical identity are not transferred.
- [Daktronics SO-2023](https://www.daktronics.com/en-us/products/sports/SO-2023) is manufacturer documentation for an LED scoreboard displaying score/time digits. It supports luminous physical numeric display capability; it does not establish an actual game, its history or any requested score. The large service-manual PDF could be found by search but failed full web open due size, so it is not a basis for new segment engineering claims.

The three candidate prose passages and their application contracts are researcher authored, not quotations. Their provenance SHA256 is the hash of this local review, never a claimed webpage digest. No semantic proof, native acceptance, image fidelity or retrieval improvement is established by this research.
