# Illustration Creative Direction

## Authorial Touch Is a Decision System

Do not use a named artist, studio, style label, texture stack, or generic `beautiful/cinematic/detailed` wording as evidence of authorial voice. Express authorship through repeatable choices:

- what receives the first and second look;
- what the frame or panel deliberately omits;
- where edges become hard, soft, lost, or unfinished;
- how mark scale or density changes by structural importance;
- how one motif or material relation changes state;
- which instant is caught or withheld;
- how the same rule survives crop, panel, or adaptation.

## Ordinary Composition

Every composed prompt needs concrete phrases for:

- `focal_hierarchy_phrase`
- `controlled_omission_phrase`
- `edge_or_mark_rule_phrase`
- `repeated_material_or_motif_rule_phrase`

The phrases must support the same event and topic. Do not add four unrelated flourishes.

## High-Creativity Development

When `creative_development_required` is true:

1. State at least three ordinary first answers and reject them.
2. Develop at least four proposals using distinct operator IDs.
3. Give each proposal a familiar anchor, exactly one changed rule, at least two visible consequences, one aboutness, and one unique signature phrase.
4. Select exactly one proposal for topic fidelity, legibility, economy, and renderability.
5. Bind only the selected anchor, changed rule, consequences, reveal, and signature to the prompt.

Before selecting, perform a renderability preflight on the proposed reveal. Bind two distinct selected-proposal consequences to the typed `second_look_plan`: one primary carrier and one fallback on a different protected locus. Declare only the inspection scales where the reveal must later pass. Compound human anatomy, tiny writing or glyphs, and overlapping multi-limb shadows require explicit risk flags and cannot be the only available carrier; the fallback must be risk-free and use a different kind when the primary is risky.

Pixel review must interpret carrier language literally. A requested broad material boundary does not pass as a thin glowing edge trace. A surface-state carrier does not pass when its light/dark boundary follows an ordinary woven border, panel seam, printed motif, or other manufactured design; the causal state must visibly interrupt or contradict the substrate pattern through moisture, residue, wear, displacement, or another observable process.

For an object-relation carrier, prefer several independent physical relations that survive reduction: a fixed support, a displaced body, a separately displaced moving part, a causal connector, and visible separation from the unfinished action. Motion lines may reinforce this evidence but cannot be its only proof. Isolate the carrier against visual rest instead of embedding it in shelves, ornaments, or repeated hardware.

Reusable operators include structural analogy, expectation inversion, absence as evidence, rule extension, temporal fold, relational reversal, functional recontextualization, and controlled impossibility.

## Motif Discipline

Use at most one motif family as a meaning-bearing system. It must appear in at least two states or placements tied to the same event. Repeated decoration without a state change, several unrelated symbols, or written explanation is not visual metaphor.

## Render Review

Inspect pixels without the prompt. Require a recognizable anchor, one primary deviation, causal consequences, a discoverable first-to-second-look path, and visible authorial decisions. Preserve the first render. Record which declared carrier and scales were attempted. If the primary cue is ambiguous, keep the changed rule and first-read event but spend the single repair on the already-declared fallback carrier. Do not enlarge or repeat the failed compound shape. If a strong model prior defeats both roles within the bounded attempts, report the limit; do not stack anomalies or select from a batch.
